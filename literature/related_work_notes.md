## 1. Vision-Language Models in Intelligent Document Processing

Traditional Intelligent Document Processing (IDP) relied on cascaded pipelines combining optical character recognition (OCR), named entity recognition (NER), and rule-based layout heuristics. The advent of spatial-aware transformers such as LayoutLMv1–v3 (Huang et al., 2022) demonstrated the power of pre-training across text, visual tokens, and 2D spatial coordinates. However, cascaded architectures suffer from error accumulation: if the OCR engine fails to recognize low-contrast or degraded text, downstream transformer heads receive corrupt input tokens without the ability to re-inspect raw pixels.

Recent Vision-Language Models (VLMs), exemplified by Qwen2.5-VL (Bai et al., 2025) and InternVL 2.0 (Chen et al., 2024), present an alternative paradigm: dense end-to-end visual reasoning directly from document images. Qwen2.5-VL introduces dynamic resolution vision transformers with native bounding box tokens (`<|box_start|>`, `<|box_end|>`), enabling direct coordinate prediction. While these models achieve state-of-the-art results on clean benchmarks such as DocVQA (Mathew et al., 2021), their reliability drops steeply when confronted with severe visual corruptions, small text, or multi-page context dependencies.

## 2. Long-Document Understanding, Multimodal Retrieval, and Spatial Grounding

While single-page document understanding is mature, real-world enterprise documents (e.g., legal contracts, financial reports, technical manuals) frequently span tens or hundreds of pages. In long-document regimes, feeding all page images directly into a VLM exceeds GPU memory constraints and causes "needle-in-a-haystack" attention dilution. 

Recent benchmarks such as MMLongBench-Doc (Wang et al., 2024), LongDocURL (Deng et al., 2025), and XL-DocBench (2026) have systematically exposed the limitations of existing VLMs on long documents:
1. Wang et al. found that proprietary VLMs (including GPT-4V and Claude-3) frequently perform worse than text-only LLMs fed OCR text on long documents due to visual token truncation.
2. Over 70% of complex questions require cross-page reasoning where evidence is scattered across disjoint sections.
3. Crucially, up to 40% of correct answers generated by current models lack verifiable visual grounding (Tito et al., 2023), indicating that models exploit language priors or spurious background tokens rather than genuine evidence.

To overcome context limits, multimodal retrieval has emerged as a key solution. ColPali (Faysse et al., 2024) demonstrated that late-interaction patch embeddings derived from VLMs (PaliGemma) dramatically outperform traditional text-based BM25 or dense text embeddings on complex visual layouts. However, existing retrieval systems operate in isolation from uncertainty calibration: they return top-$k$ candidates without assessing whether the retrieved context contains sufficient evidence to justify a definitive answer.

## 3. Uncertainty Estimation, Selective Prediction, and Abstention

In high-stakes document domains (e.g., medical records, compliance auditing, financial transactions), a hallucinated answer is far more damaging than an explicit abstention. Standard neural networks are notoriously uncalibrated and prone to overconfidence (Guo et al., 2017). In generative VLMs, this issue is exacerbated: models express high textual confidence ("I am 100% certain") even when hallucinating facts unsupported by the image.

Selective prediction (Geifman & El-Yaniv, 2017) provides a formal mathematical framework for trading off coverage for accuracy by allowing the system to abstain when uncertainty exceeds a threshold. Recent work has explored selective prediction in VQA via variational approximations (Bain et al., 2025), active evidence search (Gao et al., 2024), black-box neighborhood consistency (Ren et al., 2024), and conformal risk control (Park et al., 2025). 

However, prior work on VLM uncertainty exhibits two fundamental gaps:
1. **Domain Gap**: Almost all existing selective prediction and calibration studies focus on natural scene classification or casual VQA (ImageNet, VQA-v2, GQA). They do not address dense textual documents, where uncertainty is tightly coupled with physical document degradation and spatial layout ambiguity.
2. **Signal Isolation**: Existing approaches rely on a single uncertainty signal (e.g., token entropy or consistency across perturbations). They fail to leverage the rich, heterogeneous signals available in IDP pipelines, including OCR confidence, dense retrieval similarity scores, bounding-box spatial grounding alignment, and visual degradation quality vectors.

## 4. Document Degradation and Robustness

Document images captured in real-world scenarios (mobile phone captures, low-grade scans, fax transmissions, degraded microfilms) suffer from visual corruptions including Gaussian noise, motion and optical blur, non-uniform illumination and glare, geometric skew, compression artifacts, and physical occlusions. 

The computer vision community established rigorous methodologies for corruption robustness through ImageNet-C (Hendrycks & Dietterich, 2019), demonstrating that deep models experience catastrophic failure under mild, out-of-distribution visual noise. In the document domain, traditional pipelines combat degradation through restoration modules such as DocTr (Zhao et al., 2022) or DewarpNet. However, blindly applying heavy enhancement to all inputs is suboptimal: it incurs severe latency penalties ($5\times$ to $10\times$) and risks generating synthetic visual artifacts (hallucinated strokes) on clean documents.

Recent work by Chen et al. (2024) on Consensus Entropy demonstrated that multi-engine agreement can verify OCR outputs under degradation, but multi-VLM ensemble passes are computationally prohibitive for full documents. This motivates the need for an **adaptive, degradation-aware routing mechanism** that dynamically gauges image quality using lightweight heuristics and routes documents to targeted pathways: direct VLM execution for clean inputs, localized enhancement for moderate noise, and OCR-assisted inference for severe corruption.

## 5. Synthesis: Scientific Positioning of the Proposed System

The proposed system addresses the confluence of these four domains:
- Unlike fixed VLM or fixed OCR pipelines, it adapts processing dynamically to visual degradation (Gap 3).
- Unlike single-page or ungrounded QA systems, it scales to long documents via multimodal retrieval and enforces verifiable spatial bounding-box provenance (Gap 2).
- Unlike uncalibrated generative models that hallucinate under ambiguity, it aggregates heterogeneous multimodal signals to calibrate predictive uncertainty and abstains safely when evidence is insufficient (Gap 1).

This integrated formulation constitutes the core scientific contribution defended in our forthcoming IEEE manuscript.
